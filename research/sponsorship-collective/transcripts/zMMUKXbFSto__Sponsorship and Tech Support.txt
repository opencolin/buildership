when i was in university i worked in
tech support people would phone in with
problems with their computers and i
would walk them through it it wasn't a
great job but it paid the bills and paid
the tuition one of the most common
problems that people would call in with
was that their computer wouldn't turn on
and the most common solution was to plug
in your computer but if you told the
person that they hadn't thought of
plugging in their computer they wouldn't
accept the solution in other words when
people would phone in and say my
computer's not working it's not turning
on we could have solved the problem for
them in 10 seconds or less but if we
solve the problem too quickly people
would reject the solution so what did i
do every time somebody phoned in and
said their computer wasn't working i
would say we need to do a hard reset and
the way we do that is by unplugging the
computer from the wall and from your
computer and then we just have to access
a panel on the back of your computer to
make sure that there are no warning
lights flashing so we would have our
clients our customers take their
computer apart look for flashing lights
which of course couldn't happen because
their computer was unplugged then we
would ask them to time two minutes once
two minutes was up we had them put their
computer back together plug in the
computer plug it into the wall and
presto the hard reset solved the problem
except it wasn't the hard reset that
solved the problem it was the act of
plugging in the computer that solved the
problem so i can hear you asking what
does this have to do with sponsorship if
we solve a problem too quickly if we
make the solution to a problem
too simple and we arrive at the
conclusion that we can help someone too
quickly they lose trust and faith in our
solution coming up with the right answer
is really only half the battle showing
your client your sponsor your prospect
that you fully understand them that
you've heard them that you're listening
to them that you care about the problem
that they're experiencing is the other
half of the equation so what's the
sponsorship equivalent to the hard reset
far too often sponsorship seekers arrive
at a solution before they've even talked
to a prospect here's my sponsorship
package with three levels for you to
choose from instead what you want to do
is sit across from your sponsor and ask
them questions sit across from your
prospect and get to know what they're
trying to achieve once you're a hundred
percent sure that you know exactly what
they need and that you can help them
it's time for the hard reset ask them at
least five more questions and one of my
favorite questions to ask is what do you
mean by that no matter what the person
says to me how are you feeling today
great what do you mean by that the
question what do you mean by that when
asked in response to answers that seem
really obvious is a great way to get
people talking and get people to dig
deeper arriving at the solution arriving
at the conclusion that you can help too
quickly is a bad move in sales and
there's no quicker way to arrive at a
conclusion than to hand someone a
sponsorship package that you wrote
without ever speaking to them because
that sponsorship package makes a bunch
of assumptions and arrives at a
conclusion that you can help before even
asking them one thing about what they're
trying to achieve so spend the time with
your prospect to really fully understand
their problem give them the space and
the respect to fully explore their
problem and to feel like you've heard
them the best way to do that is to take
your time and fully understand what your
prospect is trying to achieve once you
have proven that you've fully explored
all possibilities with your prospect
then you've earned the right to tell
them to make sure their computer is
plugged in and what's more they'll agree
