## What’s New in Naming Rights Deals (2025/26 Snapshot)

Naming rights continue to evolve as brands seek long-term visibility paired with modern activation opportunities. Between 2023 and 2025, several high-profile deals reinforced the value of naming rights when paired with technology, sustainability, and community impact.

  * Crypto.com Arena has continued to expand and reinforce its naming-rights presence through digital activations and global brand exposure tied to major events. 
  * Ball Arena has sparked industry conversation around future renewable-energy and sustainability-aligned naming rights. 
  * Meanwhile, the Intuit Dome — the new home of the LA Clippers — represents a multi-billion-dollar valuation model where naming rights are deeply integrated into technology, fan experience, and data-driven engagement.

Across the industry, tech, fintech, EV manufacturers, airlines, and renewable energy brands are increasingly entering naming rights deals, viewing them as long-term brand infrastructure rather than simple signage plays.

## What Are Naming Rights?

Naming rights are like a long-term advertisement. You sell a sponsor the right to have their name put on a building, often a [sports](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/the-complete-guide-to-sport-sponsorship/) arena. Then your sponsor’s name could become the next Gillette Stadium or Wells Fargo Center. 

In 2026, naming rights deals typically range from 10–30 years for major professional venues, with shorter 3–7-year agreements remaining common for regional or emerging properties. Average pricing varies widely from six figures annually for smaller venues to tens of millions per year for top-tier stadiums.

Beyond sports, naming rights are increasingly appearing in airports, university facilities, transit systems, healthcare campuses, and mixed-use developments. This reflects broader acceptance of sponsorship-driven naming.

For a deeper breakdown of naming formats, see our [Guide to Title Sponsorship, Naming & Presenting Rights](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-title-sponsorship-naming-and-presenting-rights-defined/).

In title-sponsored naming rights, whatever the original property’s name was is completely erased in exchange for the new sponsored building title. With presenting-sponsored naming rights, you take a traditional arena name and add the brand to either the beginning or end of the name. For example, take the Hubert H. Humphrey Metrodome.

Although it doesn’t exist anymore (it’s been torn down), the Metrodome in Minneapolis had many names over the years. Back in 2009, it was called the Mall of America Field at Hubert H. Humphrey Metrodome when Mall of America bought the building’s naming rights. Whew, that name sure was a mouthful! After Mall of America’s naming rights term ended, the building went back to just being called the Hubert H. Humphrey Metrodome.

If you can’t name a building, you can always claim naming rights for a sporting event. The Division 1 college football games used traditional names for a long time, but have since begun favoring sponsored naming rights such as The Rose Bowl Game presented by Northwestern Mutual or the Capital One Orange Bowl. Some college division games have embraced title-sponsored naming rights. As an example, the Camping World Bowl once was the Tangerine Bowl.

Naming rights deals, when done right, can be hugely, hugely valuable. I’m talking multi-million-dollar deals in some cases. So far, the most expensive naming rights deal in history was that between the Bank of Nova Scotia in Canada and the Maple Leaf Sports and Entertainment company. In 2017, the two businesses penned a deal worth CAD $800 million that would last for 20 years. In American dollars, that’s $635.84 million. 

I do want to make it clear: not all naming right deals need to be so enormously expensive. Smaller deals for lesser-known stadiums can be just as mutually beneficial to the [sponsor](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/the-anatomy-of-a-great-sponsorship-program/) and your sports team or organization.

### Know Your Budget

Of all the assets you can offer a [target sponsor](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/start-before-you-are-ready/), naming rights may be among the most appealing, but they’re also one of the most expensive. Your sports team must have the budget for naming rights, and not just now, but in the future as well.

In 2025/26, regional venues often command low-to-mid six figures annually, while major professional venues range from high six figures to eight figures per year depending on market size and exposure.

Your budget must account not only for naming rights value, but also signage upgrades, digital integrations, legal fees, and ongoing activation requirements.

You’ll be the one to present the terms of the sponsorship naming rights deal, so think about it for a while. If you can only afford three years of naming rights, that’s okay, as it’s better than nothing. Just be clear about that in your sponsorship package. It’s also fine if you can’t provide the target sponsor naming rights to an arena with a capacity of 65,000+ people like Gillette Stadium. 

I can’t stress enough how important it is to be realistic when budgeting for sponsorship naming rights. I say this on the blog a lot, but one of the worst things you can do in sponsorship is overpromise and underdeliver. 

If you’re honest about what you can offer a sponsor, sooner or later, someone will bite. Yet if you promise too much, get a big-name sponsor, and then fail to live up to the naming rights you said you’d provide, you lose out on that sponsor. You also could be in breach of contract with the sponsor. The financial repercussions, as well as the ding to your reputation, can spell the end of your sports team.

### Choose the Building Smartly 

In naming rights sponsorship, you have to look into the crystal ball often and predict what the future will hold. That especially comes into play when selecting which building will be the one that your sponsor will provide naming rights for. Sure, a prediction is just that and not a guarantee, but you must have at least a decent idea of what’s coming down the pike.

Many modern naming rights deals now focus on multipurpose venues that host sports, concerts, esports, and corporate events, dramatically increasing annual exposure.

Key considerations now include:

  * How many broadcast events occur annually?
  * Is the venue part of a larger entertainment district?
  * Confirm no redevelopment or demolition is planned within the contract window

If you promise your sponsor 10 years of naming rights and two years into the deal, you’re approached by the building contractors and told that the arena is being torn down, you’re now up the creek without a paddle. You promised the sponsor eight more years and they’re not going to get it.

Unless you can come up with a similar building in a very short amount of time, then you again risk being in breach of contract. 

When choosing a building for naming rights, you want to look at more than just the cost. How old is the building? Has it been recently renovated? Is it still a popular place? Are there plans to expand around the stadium, possibly adding a second arena? The answers to these questions will determine how viable a building you’ve selected. 

### Recognize That a Name Is More Than Just That

When you provide naming rights to a sponsor, you’re selling them more than simply a title on a basketball stadium. The names of these buildings become interwoven into the fabric of the city or town in which the stadium resides. This provides ample opportunity for partnerships with local retailers and others within the community. 

Modern naming rights frequently include community impact programs such as youth sports funding, STEM initiatives, or local education partnerships, which sponsors increasingly value.

Additionally, venue names now appear across Google Maps, ticketing platforms, rideshare apps, and event calendars, delivering immediate and ongoing digital brand exposure.

## Have the Right Contractual Provisions

I have to talk about the legalities of sponsorship naming rights as well. In your contract, you should offer the sponsor exclusivity provisions to further sweeten the deal. These provisions prevent the local competition from sponsoring and/or advertising in a way that detracts from the sponsor’s naming rights.

You’ll need to provide prominence provisions as well, sometimes referred to as dominance provisions. As those names suggest, a prominence provision ensures that all advertising and signage for the sponsor is displayed more often than any other sponsors. Their digital displays will outsize all advertising and shine more brightly, quite literally in this instance. The placement of the signage will be recurring and highly visible as well per the prominence provisions. 

Modern agreements also include clauses for:

  * Digital signage dominance
  * AR/VR broadcast overlays
  * Brand integration within venue apps and ticketing systems

For legal structure and clause examples, see our guide on [Building Naming Rights Agreements.](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/blog/how-to-create-a-building-naming-rights-agreement/)

### Understand How Naming Rights Payments May Work 

Naming rights sponsorship payments don’t always occur upfront. Instead, if you score a high-dollar deal, the sponsor might make payment installments throughout the deal’s term. So if the sponsor bought six-figure or even seven-figure naming rights, it can take upwards of 10, 20, sometimes even 30 years before all those zeros are in your sports team’s bank account. 

In 2025/26, many deals use step-up payment structures, annual escalators (typically 2–5%), equity components, or community investment commitments tied to performance milestones.

Talk to your sponsor about this, as many sponsors have found workarounds so more of the money is in the sports team’s pocket faster. Maybe the sponsor pays through bonds if that’s possible.

## Naming-Rights Evaluation Checklist (For Sponsors & Properties)

Naming rights deals work best when both sides clearly understand what they’re evaluating beyond just the price tag. This checklist helps sponsors and properties quickly assess whether a naming rights opportunity is strategically sound and likely to deliver long-term ROI.

### For Properties (Sponsees)

  * Is the venue structurally and operationally stable long-term, with no planned demolition or major redevelopment during the contract window?
  * Does the venue attract consistent annual attendance and regular media coverage?
  * Can you guarantee signage prominence and category exclusivity throughout the agreement?
  * Is community sentiment toward the venue and team generally positive?
  * Are replacement naming options or co-branded solutions available if conditions change?

### For Sponsors

  * Does the venue’s audience align with your target customer base and geographic priorities?
  * Is the projected cost per impression competitive compared to other media investments?
  * Are digital extensions included, such as AR signage, venue app placements, broadcast mentions, or social amplification?
  * Is the event calendar strong enough (sports, concerts, special events) to sustain long-term ROI?
  * Are there opportunities to align the naming rights with community initiatives, CSR goals, or sustainability programs?

When both parties can confidently check most of these boxes, naming rights shift from a branding gamble to a long-term strategic asset.
